The DIBELS risk calculator turns raw DIBELS scores into risk levels. You upload a CSV, and hFlow gives you back the same file with one extra column called **Risk Level**. It saves you from looking up each score in the printed benchmark tables by hand.

This tool is switched on for individual people rather than for a whole school, so you only see it if it has been enabled for your account. If you expect to see it and do not, contact hFlow support.

<Note>
The calculator does not save anything. Your uploaded file and the results are not stored in hFlow, and rows are not matched to the students already on your roster. Think of it as a lookup tool: scores in, risk levels out.
</Note>

## What you need in your file

Your file must be a **CSV**, with a header row and up to **1,000** data rows. If your scores are in Excel, save a copy as CSV first (File, then Save As, then choose CSV).

It must contain these six columns. The names are flexible (see [Column names](#column-names)), but the information has to be there.

| Column | What to put in it |
|---|---|
| Student first name | The student's first name |
| Student last name | The student's last name |
| Grade | `K` through `8` |
| Test | Which DIBELS measure was given, for example `LNF` or `ORF-WC` |
| Period | `Beg`, `Mid`, or `End` |
| Result | The score, as a number |

Grade and period both accept the longer spellings you are likely to already have in an export. Grade takes `K`, `Kindergarten`, `Grade 3`, `3rd`, or `THIRD`. Period takes `Beg`, `Beginning`, or `BOY` for the beginning of the year, and the equivalents for the middle and end. Season names such as `Winter` are not accepted, because which period they mean depends on the school calendar.

You can include any other columns you like. Student IDs, teacher names, class names, notes: all of them come back untouched in the download, in the same order.

### Test names

Use one of these in the **Test** column. Capitalization does not matter, and the full name works as well as the code.

| Code | Full name |
|---|---|
| Composite | DIBELS Composite Score |
| LNF | Letter Naming Fluency |
| PSF | Phonemic Segmentation Fluency |
| NWF-CLS | Nonsense Word Fluency: Correct Letter Sounds |
| NWF-WRC | Nonsense Word Fluency: Whole Words Read |
| WRF | Word Reading Fluency |
| ORF-WC | Oral Reading Fluency: Words Correct |
| ORF-Accuracy | Oral Reading Fluency: Accuracy |
| Maze | Maze Adjusted Score |

Write `NWF-CLS` or `NWF-WRC` rather than plain `NWF`, and `ORF-WC` or `ORF-Accuracy` rather than plain `ORF`. Each of those pairs has its own benchmark table, so a bare `NWF` or `ORF` could mean either one. Rather than guess, the calculator flags the row and tells you which two options to choose from.

### Column names

The calculator recognizes common variations in the column headings themselves, so you usually do not need to rename anything. For example, all of these work for the score column: `Result`, `Score`, `Value`. Grade can be `Grade`, `Grade Level`, or `Year`. Test can be `Test`, `Assessment`, or `Measure`.

Extra spaces and capitalization are ignored, and your columns can be in any order.

## How to calculate risk levels

1. Open **Assessments** in the top navigation and choose **DIBELS calculator**.
2. Under **Benchmark edition**, leave the default edition selected unless you have been told to use a different one.
3. Under **Score file**, choose your CSV file.
4. Click **Calculate risk levels**.

Your browser downloads the finished file straight away, named after your original file with `risk-levels` and the edition added, for example `winter-screening-risk-levels-dibels8.csv`. You can open that CSV in Excel, Google Sheets, or Numbers.

A short summary appears on the page underneath the button telling you how many rows were scored and how many did not match a benchmark. If some rows did not match, the **Risk Level** column in the file explains why for each one.

If you upload a file that the calculator already processed, the existing **Risk Level** column is refreshed in place rather than a second one being added.

## Reading the results

Rows that match a benchmark get a risk level made up of the band, the level of risk, and the recommended support:

| Risk Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| `Blue - Negligible Risk (Core Support)` | Well above the benchmark |
| `Green - Minimal Risk (Core Support)` | At or above the benchmark |
| `Yellow - Some Risk (Strategic Support)` | Below the benchmark, some risk |
| `Red - At Risk (Intensive Support)` | Well below the benchmark, at risk |

Not every test has all four bands at every grade and time of year. Letter Naming Fluency and ORF Accuracy have no Blue band, for instance, and a few Kindergarten cells only have two. That comes from the published benchmark document, not from hFlow.

## When a row does not get a risk level

Instead of a single unhelpful "not found", the **Risk Level** column tells you exactly what was wrong with the row, so you can fix it and upload again.

| Risk Level | What to do |
|---|---|
| `grade not found: 11` | The grade is outside K through 8. Check the grade in your source data. |
| `grade not found: (empty)` | The grade cell is blank. |
| `test not found: Reading Fluency` | The test name is not one hFlow recognizes. Use a name from the [Test names](#test-names) table. |
| `test is ambiguous: ORF (use ORF-WC or ORF-Accuracy)` | Say which of the two ORF measures you mean. |
| `test is ambiguous: NWF (use NWF-CLS or NWF-WRC)` | Say which of the two NWF measures you mean. |
| `period not found: Winter` | The period must be `Beg`, `Mid`, or `End`, or one of their longer spellings. Season names are not accepted. |
| `invalid result: n/a` | The score is not a number. Blank cells, text, and negative numbers all land here. |
| `Test not administered for this grade/period` | That test has no benchmark at that grade and time of year, for example Letter Naming Fluency in Grade 4. Check that the grade and test on the row belong together. |
| `Below lowest benchmark` | The score sits under the lowest published range for that test, grade, and period. Usually this means the score is in the wrong column. |
| `Score between benchmarks` | The score falls in a gap between two published ranges. This is rare. Contact hFlow support with the row if you see it. |

Rows are checked in order: grade first, then test, then period, then the score. If a row has more than one problem, you see the first one, so a row can need a second look after you fix the first issue.

## Where the benchmarks come from

Risk levels come from the official DIBELS benchmark goals published by the University of Oregon. hFlow does not adjust the numbers.

Two ways to check what the calculator is using:

- **Download benchmark PDF** on the calculator page gives you the original published document, exactly as it was issued.
- **View benchmarks** opens a table of every range hFlow has loaded, which you can filter by edition, grade, and test.

On the benchmarks table, a range marked with an asterisk (`*`) is one where the printed document has an evident typo, such as a range printed backwards. Hover over the asterisk to see the printed value and why it was corrected. The PDF you download is never altered.

<Note>
DIBELS benchmark goals are published by the University of Oregon. The **Benchmark edition** dropdown exists because the goals are revised from time to time. Unless someone tells you otherwise, use the edition marked as the default.
</Note>

## Admin vs teacher

Access to the calculator does not follow the usual roles. Being an owner or admin does not give you the DIBELS calculator, and having access to **Assessments** is not required either. It is enabled per person by hFlow support, so a teacher can have it while an admin in the same school does not.
